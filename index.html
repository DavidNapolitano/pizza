How this works · cooling down & warming up

Each stage uses up part of the fermentation: its hours ÷ the chart hours at that temperature for a given yeast column. Following Craig's multi-temperature method, every stage is converted to equivalent hours at the Stage 1 temperature and the published yeast % whose Stage 1 chart time is nearest is chosen (ties go to the lower %). A single stage is a plain chart lookup. Nothing is extrapolated.

Temperatures are ambient (air), as in the chart. Below 40 °F = cold fermentation (CF), 40–54 °F = intermediate (INT), 55–80 °F = room temperature (RT).

Cool-down (RT → CT): dough takes a few hours to chill, so it ferments a little faster at first than the CT row assumes.

Warm-up (CT → RT): the reverse: cold dough ferments slower than the RT row assumes until it warms up. Enter the warm-up as its own stage at room temperature (the RT → CT → RT preset does this). That slightly overstates the warm-up's fermentation, so the calculator leans toward a little less yeast.

The two effects partly cancel and are small next to the chart's cold-fermentation error. For more precision, split a transition into a short in-between stage at the temperature you actually measure.
